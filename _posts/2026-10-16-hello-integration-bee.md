## what is an integration bee?

For those who don't know, an integration bee is essentially a competition where people fight to see who can solve integrals the fastest. Two people face off on blackboards live in front of a relatively stinky audience for some kind of prize, usually some math book, merch, or best case scenario, money. These are your standard integrals either, it's not just going to be some tricky u-sub problem like 

$$\int \frac{dx}{\sqrt{x}+\sqrt[3]{x}}$$

(hint: u-sub so the power clears both radicals at once). Here's one from the MIT 2026 **qualifying round**:

$$\int_0^{1000} \left( \lfloor \lceil x \rceil \rfloor + \lceil \lfloor x \rfloor \rceil + \lfloor \{x\} \rfloor + \{ \lfloor x \rfloor \} + \lceil \{x\} \rceil + \{ \lceil x \rceil \} \right) dx$$

Dude! What the heck! Ahhhhh!

MIT started the hype really by putting their integration bees on youtube, which are sometimes really fun to watch because these people are amazing. I will likely use their problems as practice, but Berkeley releases some of theirs too. Berkeley's Integration Bee is hosted jointly by the Society of Physics Students and the Mathematics Undergrad Student Association (I am in both!) some time around mid-April to May. This gives me around six months of solid time to practice between midterms, homework, rowing, and whatever club stuff I have next semester.
